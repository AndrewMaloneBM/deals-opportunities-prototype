## TL;DR

The workshop confirmed that low deal adoption is not only a visibility problem. Sellers also need:

More predictable target prices and deal conditions

Earlier visibility of upcoming deals and relevant models

A clear understanding of the financial benefit of participating

- The ability to act through their existing workflows, including API, CSV and pricing tools

Better visibility of progress, performance and refunds during and after a deal

These findings are consistent with the 2025 SellerXP research, which identified the same needs around credible economics, lead time, clear eligibility, workflow compatibility and performance tracking.

## Main findings

Discover

Some sellers miss deals entirely or only learn about them through the Back Office or direct SSM outreach. Earlier visibility of the product scope, models and target prices would help sellers plan their sourcing and

inventory.

### Assess

Sellers need to understand whether a deal is commercially worthwhile. They want clearer information about:

- Target price

- Commission or rebate

- Expected margin impact

- Deal duration

- Required volume or conditions

- Expected financial benefit

Showing only price proximity or price decrease is not enough.

### Act

Manual work is still important for SSMs, including:

- Explaining deals to sellers

- Identifying “near miss” opportunities

- Helping sellers calculate the financial impact

- Sharing deal information and price updates

Some integrated sellers also face a workflow problem: their automated pricing systems can overwrite deal prices, which may create a financial risk if the rebate is not correctly taken into account.

### Track

Sellers have limited visibility of:

- Their progress during a deal

- Deal adoption and performance

- Volume or GMV generated through a deal

- Expected refunds or commission benefits

- Whether the deal was financially worthwhile

This is particularly problematic for volume-based incentives, where sellers may not know how close they are to meeting the requirements.

## Initial conclusions

The group aligned on the following directions:

1. Do not prioritise volume-based incentives for the current Deals V1 direction. Sellers find them difficult to understand and track, and they create uncertainty when sourcing inventory.

2. Make the financial benefit clearer. The listing page should help sellers understand the monetary benefit of aligning with a target price, rather than showing only the price difference or proximity to the target.

3. Reduce target price volatility. More stable target prices would give sellers greater confidence when planning inventory and sourcing stock.

4. Consider earlier deal visibility. Sellers need enough lead time to understand the opportunity and take

   - action.

These are initial directions for the next stage of discovery.

## Suggestions to validate

Ahead of the workshop, Yini shared several ideas for improving the seller experience.

|Stage|Suggestion|
|---|---|
|Discover|Improve visibility of the weekly or monthly product scope|
|Assess|Clearly communicate target prices|
|Assess|Show commission-rate changes separately for always-on and exclusive deals|
|Act|Flag deal-eligible listings directly in the seller workflow|
|Act|Provide a masterfile or view showing profitability for deal-eligible listings|

## Additional seller feedback after the workshop

Nefix International (6163EU), UK seller, 250k-300k monthly GMV

I would also like to raise a concern regarding the wording of your selling deals, particularly the phrase “up to 4% discount.” We find this quite ambiguous, and it makes it difficult for sellers to understand what the actual final charges will be when compared with the standard commission rate of 11%.

For example, letʼs say the deal gives us a 4% discount, effectively reducing the commission from 11% to 7%. On the face of it, this appears to be a significant reduction in fees. However, in reality, this can be somewhat misleading because the amount we are required to reduce the selling price by can be very significant. As a result, although the commission appears lower, we may ultimately receive substantially less from the final selling price.

The current arrangement also makes it difficult for us to assess the deal because the discount is not reflected in the individual transactions and only appears a month later. This leaves us having to guess at the time of sale:

what the actual commission/fees will be;

how much we should reduce our selling price;

whether it is financially worthwhile for us to accept a particular deal; and what the final amount we will actually receive will be.

We believe there should be a much simpler and more transparent fixed-fee structure, clearly communicated to sellers in advance. This would allow us to understand the exact cost and the actual net amount we will receive before deciding whether to participate in a particular deal.

At present, the headline “up to 4% discount” can give the impression that sellers are receiving a substantial fee reduction, when the overall financial impact on the seller may be considerably less favourable once the required reduction in the selling price is taken into account.
